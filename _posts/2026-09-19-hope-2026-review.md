> **⚠️ SPOILERS AHEAD:** This review discusses major plot points, the creatures, the ending, and the post-credit scene of *Hope (2026)*.

![Hope (2026) poster](https://image.starnewskorea.com/21/2026/05/2026052910143964338_1.jpg)

## We finally watched it together

I watched the Korean movie *Hope* on **19 September 2026 with my sister**.

There is actually a little story behind that.

A few days earlier, we had originally planned to watch *Hope* together, but my sister was sick, so we couldn't go. That ended up being the reason I went to watch *Resident Evil* by myself instead.

I mentioned that in my [*Resident Evil* review]({% post_url 2026-09-19-resident-evil-2026-review %}), so it felt quite funny to finally sit down with my sister and watch the movie we originally planned to see.

And after all that waiting, *Hope* turned out to be a very strange movie.

Not bad strange.

Just... **what exactly am I watching?** strange.

Directed by **Na Hong-jin**, *Hope* is a South Korean science-fiction thriller starring Hwang Jung-min as police chief Beom-seok, Jo In-sung as hunter Seong-gi and Jung Ho-yeon as police officer Seong-ae. The theatrical cut runs for **156 minutes**, or about 2 hours and 36 minutes. citeturn0search0turn3search3

And yes, the movie really does start with people looking for what they think is a tiger.

It gets considerably weirder from there.

## It starts with a dead cow

The beginning is actually quite simple.

Beom-seok, the local police chief, gets involved after a cow is found brutally killed.

Seong-gi and the hunters are around the area, and the first impression is basically that some kind of large animal — possibly a tiger — has attacked it.

![The hunters in the forest](https://www.koreadaily.com/data/photo/2026/07/20/da68b2f9-f787-47b3-98f4-3b6e7ff0a63c.jpg)

*The forest scenes do a good job of making the hunters look like they are searching for an animal rather than an alien.*

The police and hunters start investigating, but slowly the situation becomes much bigger than a dead cow.

People are attacked.

Buildings are damaged.

There are strange tracks and signs that something very powerful is moving around the village.

And eventually the police and hunters split up to investigate from different directions.

This was probably the part of the movie I enjoyed the most.

The movie takes its time making me wonder **what is actually attacking these people**.

## I liked how the movie builds the environment

One thing *Hope* does well for me is the setup.

The village feels isolated.

The police don't have an easy way to call for help, the hunters are relying on guns and horses, and everyone is trying to understand a threat that they don't really understand.

That makes the creature feel much more dangerous than if the movie had simply opened with:

> "Aliens have arrived. Everybody run."

Instead, we get this slow escalation.

A cow is dead.

Then people are dead.

Then something is running around.

Then the police are chasing it.

Then suddenly we're dealing with something completely different.

This is also something other reviews noticed. Yonhap described the first part as following the characters as they track an unseen presence, with the creature mostly being suggested through sounds, footprints and the aftermath of attacks. citeturn1search0

## The monster takes forever to properly show up

This is where I had mixed feelings.

I liked the mystery.

But I also felt like the movie was **dragging the reveal a little too much**.

The creature is kept mostly out of sight for quite a long time. Even the promotional material leaned into that mystery, with characters pointing their guns into a forest where you can't really see what they're looking at. citeturn2search6

Eventually we finally get a proper look.

And at that point, I was thinking:

**Oh. So this is an alien movie.**

Not a tiger movie.

Not a giant-animal movie.

Aliens.

The film takes roughly the first hour to fully reveal the creature, something that has also been noted in reviews of the film. citeturn2search12turn3search7

I understand why the director wanted to hold the reveal back. It makes the first part feel like a mystery.

But because the movie is already **156 minutes long**, I did wonder whether some of that waiting could have been shortened.

For me, the mystery was interesting.

The waiting was occasionally less interesting.

## Then the creature runs into the city

Once the creature gets into the more populated area, the movie becomes much more chaotic.

This is where Seong-ae, played by Jung Ho-yeon, becomes much more important.

![Seong-ae in the police car](https://cineplay-cms.s3.amazonaws.com/article-images/2026-05-24/15fc0a02-69f0-49e6-960c-1b6665dd1b31.jpg?v=2)

*Seong-ae looking like she has already had a very long day.*

I liked the way the movie moves from the forest into the village and roads.

Suddenly the creature isn't just something the hunters are searching for.

Now it is something the police are trying to stop while people are panicking and everything around them is falling apart.

There are car chases, gunfights and people desperately trying to work out where the creature is going.

And then the movie gives us something I wasn't really expecting.

The creature **cries**.

## The monster wasn't simply hunting people

This was probably the part that made me feel the most sorry for the creature.

At first, I was basically watching it as the monster.

It attacks people.

It destroys things.

It looks terrifying.

So naturally, I was thinking:

**Okay, we need to stop this thing.**

But later, when it is being chased by the police, the creature is crying and desperately moving around as if it is searching for something.

Then we learn about the dead alien child.

The villagers had unknowingly become involved in something much bigger, and a child had been killed and hidden away.

That completely changed how I looked at the creature.

Suddenly the monster wasn't just rampaging because it wanted to kill humans.

It was looking for its lost child.

![The alien creature in the forest](https://www.flickeringmyth.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Hope-1.jpg)

*The movie eventually gives us a much clearer look at the alien creatures.*

I actually felt quite sad when the creature died in the truck crash.

And then the movie makes things even darker by showing that the creature ends up becoming part of the human investigation and experimentation.

At that point I was thinking:

**Okay, maybe humans are not exactly the innocent victims here either.**

## There isn't just one alien

This was another reveal that made me rethink the whole movie.

There isn't simply one giant alien creature that arrived to destroy the village.

There are multiple creatures.

And more importantly, they aren't just generic monsters.

They are **intelligent beings**.

That part was interesting to me because I initially expected something much simpler.

You know the usual alien movie setup:

> Aliens arrive.

> Aliens hate humans.

> Aliens destroy everything.

> Humans shoot aliens.

But *Hope* isn't quite doing that.

The creatures have their own society, their own relationships and their own reasons for being there.

The movie eventually expands the story beyond the village and shows that what is happening is connected to a much larger alien situation. Even the official synopsis describes the story as growing from a local mystery into a much larger conflict involving "otherworldly beings." citeturn3search3

## But then I started asking: why don't they just communicate?

This is probably where the script became a little weird for me.

If these aliens are intelligent enough to have their own society, spaceships and plans, then I kept wondering:

**Why can't they just communicate with the humans?**

I know the obvious answer is that they don't speak human language.

And the movie deliberately keeps their conversations mysterious for most of the runtime.

But from my perspective, this creates a funny situation where both sides are basically misunderstanding each other while shooting at each other.

The humans think:

**Monster! Shoot it!**

The aliens are basically thinking:

**Where is our missing child?**

And then everything escalates.

That actually fits one of the movie's larger ideas: a tragedy can grow because people don't understand what the other side is doing.

Still, I couldn't stop thinking that if the aliens were intelligent enough to communicate somehow, maybe the whole situation could have gone very differently.

## The hunter with the cap becomes the action hero

After most of the hunter team gets wiped out, Seong-gi is basically left on his own.

And this is where the movie goes completely into action mode.

![Seong-gi riding through the forest](https://images.gram.pl/news/bdtd20260518125658323fmku.jpg)

*Seong-gi on horseback. This is probably the most "Korean alien western" image I could have asked for.*

The movie suddenly gives us **a guy on a horse fighting an alien that can transform into a tiger-like creature**.

And honestly?

That part was pretty cool.

Seong-gi is riding through the forest while the alien is chasing him.

He is firing his weapon from horseback.

The creature is moving through the trees.

Everything is happening at ridiculous speed.

According to coverage of the film, Jo In-sung performed demanding horseback action for the role, including sequences through forests and on roads. citeturn2search0

This was one of those moments where I stopped questioning the logic and just watched.

**Horse versus alien tiger.**

Sure.

Let's go.

## Then the movie kills him... or does it?

Eventually Seong-gi manages to help slow down the tiger-like alien.

After everything the hunters have been through, it becomes a proper one-on-one showdown.

And then...

Seong-gi falls.

He is thrown around, badly injured, and eventually appears to die.

The scene just sort of ends with everyone looking confused and silent.

I was sitting there thinking:

**Wait... that's it?**

Because this guy had already survived an absolutely ridiculous amount of punishment.

He gets thrown into trees.

He gets injured.

He gets chased.

He fights an alien.

He survives things that should probably have ended the movie for him several times already.

So when he finally falls down, it almost felt less like a dramatic death and more like:

**The movie has decided this is the moment he dies.**

Then the post-credit scene happens.

And yep.

He's alive.

## Seong-gi definitely has some plot armour

I'm not complaining too much because the post-credit reveal made me laugh.

But come on.

This guy had suffered multiple injuries, including getting thrown around through the forest, and somehow he is still walking around after the whole thing.

That is some serious plot armour.

The funny thing is that the movie actually knows this is absurd.

The post-credit scene basically confirms that the apparent death wasn't really the end for him.

So when the credits ended and he showed up alive, my reaction was less:

**Amazing!**

and more:

**I knew it.**

Still, I'm glad they did it.

After watching him survive all that punishment, it would have felt slightly ridiculous to suddenly kill him with one final fall.

## The ending made me more confused than satisfied

By the end, *Hope* had changed from:

**"What killed the cow?"**

to:

**"There are intelligent aliens searching for their child and humanity has accidentally made the situation much worse."**

That is a pretty huge jump.

And I actually liked that the movie tried something different.

But I also think the story could have explained some things more clearly.

The biggest example is the alien communication.

For most of the movie, we don't really understand what the aliens are saying. The film only gives us much clearer access to their dialogue very late in the story.

I understand the artistic reason for doing this.

But personally, I sometimes felt like I was missing information rather than deliberately being kept in suspense.

There are also several moments where I was thinking:

**Wait, how did we get from that scene to this one?**

That isn't necessarily a bad thing for a strange science-fiction movie, but it did make the final stretch feel quite messy to me.

## I liked the weirdness more than I liked the explanation

That is probably the best way I can describe my experience with *Hope*.

I liked the **idea** of the movie.

I liked the atmosphere.

I liked the slow mystery at the beginning.

I liked the way the village gradually becomes a disaster zone.

I liked that the creature was not simply an evil monster.

And I especially liked the horse-versus-alien sequence.

But once the movie started explaining the bigger alien story, I found myself asking more questions instead of getting answers.

Maybe that is intentional.

Na Hong-jin has described the movie as something that doesn't fit neatly into one genre, with aliens, action, suspense and other elements all mixed together. citeturn2search5

For me, that mixture is both the movie's strength and its weakness.

## What I liked

- The slow buildup from a dead cow to a full-blown alien disaster.
- The way the movie creates an isolated environment before everything goes crazy.
- Keeping the main creature hidden for a long time.
- The monster's emotional side once we understand what it is actually looking for.
- The idea that the aliens are intelligent rather than generic "destroy Earth" monsters.
- Jung Ho-yeon's police officer scenes.
- The forest atmosphere.
- The horseback chase and showdown with the tiger-like alien.
- The fact that the movie keeps changing what I thought the story was about.
- The post-credit scene making me realise Seong-gi wasn't actually dead.

## What didn't work as much for me

The biggest issue for me was the **length**.

At 156 minutes, *Hope* is a long movie. citeturn0search0

I don't mind long movies when the pacing keeps me interested, but here I felt the slow creature reveal could have been trimmed a little.

The other problem was the explanation of the aliens.

I actually liked discovering that they were intelligent.

I just wasn't always convinced by how the movie communicated that information.

I kept thinking about how different everything might have been if the humans and aliens had been able to understand each other earlier.

And finally, Seong-gi's survival is funny but also makes his earlier "death" feel less meaningful.

## Final thoughts

I'm glad I finally got to watch *Hope* with my sister after we had to postpone it because she was sick.

That made the whole thing feel like a little continuation of my previous movie night rather than just another random movie.

And *Hope* was definitely an experience.

It starts as a mystery about a dead cow.

Then it becomes a monster movie.

Then it becomes an alien movie.

Then it becomes a family tragedy.

Then suddenly there is a guy on horseback fighting an alien tiger.

And somehow the guy survives a death scene because of a post-credit scene.

I don't think everything in the script worked for me.

The movie is long, some of the explanations felt strange, and I think the creature reveal could have been paced a little better.

But I appreciate that it wasn't just another simple alien-invasion movie.

The part that stayed with me was actually the creature crying while being chased.

Once I understood that it might have been looking for its lost child, the whole "monster" started feeling a lot more tragic.

Maybe that is what *Hope* wanted me to think about in the first place.

Sometimes the thing everyone is trying to kill isn't necessarily the thing that started the problem.

**My experience: I liked parts of it, but I'm still quite confused by it.**

I enjoyed the atmosphere and the action more than I enjoyed trying to understand the bigger alien mythology.

And yes, I would still like to know what happens next.

Mostly because after that post-credit scene, I'm now wondering how on earth Seong-gi survived all that.
