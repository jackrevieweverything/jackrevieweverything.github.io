## Thomas from The Maze Runner vs. Giant Bugs

When I saw **Dylan O'Brien**, the guy I remember as Thomas from *The Maze Runner*, was starring in *Love and Monsters*, I already had one thought:

**We can't go wrong with this, right?**

Well, after finally watching it on Netflix, I can say the movie is quite a fun one.

It is also a little unfortunate when you remember when it came out.

*Love and Monsters* was released in 2020, right in the middle of the COVID-19 period. Paramount moved it away from its planned theatrical release and released it through video-on-demand in October 2020, while Netflix brought it to international audiences in April 2021. So this was very much a movie that arrived when going to the cinema was not exactly a normal thing. [Paramount](https://www.paramountpictures.com/movies/love-and-monsters) lists the 2020 release, while Netflix's media page notes the international Netflix release in April 2021.

I actually find that unfortunate because this feels like the kind of movie that could have been a pretty entertaining cinema experience.

Big monsters.

Big explosions.

A giant crab.

A giant frog.

A giant centipede.

A giant... basically-everything.

Instead, it became one of those movies people could easily miss while sitting at home during lockdown.

And I only got around to watching it recently.

## Joel Is Not Exactly the Hero Type

The movie starts with Joel Dawson living underground with a group of survivors.

Everyone else seems much more useful than him when it comes to fighting monsters.

Joel?

Not so much.

When danger appears, he freezes.

Literally.

There is an early scene where the bunker is attacked and Joel is supposed to help defend everyone. Instead, he completely locks up when faced with a monster.

Luckily, one of his bunker mates deals with it before Joel becomes monster food.

This is actually a good way to introduce Joel.

He isn't the typical post-apocalyptic hero who wakes up every morning ready to shoot something.

He is a guy who can cook, maintain things and help around the bunker, but when a giant creature appears, his brain basically says:

**Nope. We're done here.**

And then he hears over the radio that his old girlfriend, **Aimee**, is still alive.

That changes everything.

## The World's Most Dangerous Boyfriend Journey

Joel decides he is going to leave the bunker and travel roughly 80 miles across a monster-infested surface world to find Aimee.

There is only one small problem.

**He can't actually kill monsters.**

He isn't properly trained.

He doesn't have much experience outside.

And he is basically walking into a world where the insects and amphibians have evolved into things that look like they belong in a nightmare.

But Joel goes anyway.

For me, this is where the movie's main idea starts to work.

It isn't really about Joel suddenly becoming brave.

It is about him deciding that being afraid isn't enough of a reason to stay underground forever.

And honestly, I have to give the guy credit.

He knows he is scared.

He knows he is unprepared.

He still goes.

## The Giant Frog Almost Ends the Movie Early

Not long after leaving, Joel encounters a giant frog/toad creature.

It is huge.

It is disgusting.

It has a ridiculous tongue.

And when Joel gets attacked by it, I honestly thought:

**Well, that's the end of Joel.**

Fortunately, he gets rescued by one of the movie's best characters.

A dog.

His name is **Boy**.

And Boy is immediately more useful than Joel.

Boy saves Joel from the giant toad and then basically decides to stick around.

I really liked the relationship between these two.

Joel is supposed to be the hero, but Boy is the one constantly warning him about danger.

Poisonous food?

Boy knows.

Monsters nearby?

Boy knows.

Joel doing something stupid?

Boy probably knows that too.

## Boy Has His Own Little Home

One of the things I liked about Boy is that he isn't just randomly following Joel around.

Joel eventually discovers that Boy has been living inside an abandoned bus.

The bus has an interesting rope mechanism for opening the door, presumably something created by the previous owner.

And that immediately made me wonder what happened to Boy's original owner.

The movie doesn't spend too much time explaining every little detail, but I liked that it gave Boy a little bit of history.

He isn't just:

**Cute dog appears because the movie needs a cute dog.**

He has clearly survived on his own for a long time.

And he is carrying around the red dress that belonged to his former owner.

That little detail actually makes Boy's character surprisingly emotional underneath all the comedy.

## Then Joel Falls Into a Pit of Worms

Just when I thought Joel had survived the worst of it, he falls into a pit filled with these horrible worm-like creatures called **Sand Gobblers**.

At this point I was thinking:

**Yep. He's dead.**

Again.

This guy somehow keeps finding new ways to almost die.

Luckily, he is rescued by **Clyde** and **Minnow**.

Clyde is played by Michael Rooker, and he immediately feels like the experienced apocalypse survivor Joel desperately needs.

Minnow, played by Ariana Greenblatt, is much younger but is also far more capable than Joel.

Together they basically give Joel a crash course in:

**How not to get eaten.**

## Clyde and Minnow's Survival Lessons

I couldn't remember all of Clyde's rules either, so I looked them up after watching the movie.

The movie doesn't present them as one neat numbered survival manual, but these are the main lessons I remembered from their conversations and Joel's journey:

- **Good instincts come from making mistakes and surviving them.**
- **Not every monster wants to kill you.** Clyde teaches Joel to watch the creature's eyes and learn its behaviour.
- **Camping has two basic rules: you can eat or you can sleep. You can't comfortably do both.** Joel obviously hates this rule.
- **The colder mountains are safer**, because many of the monsters are less active there.
- **Learn the creatures instead of assuming every one of them is an enemy.**
- **Pay attention to what you eat.** Boy repeatedly proves that knowing which plants and berries are poisonous can save your life.
- **Use your environment and your knowledge instead of trying to fight everything.**
- And perhaps the bigger lesson: **don't settle just because you're afraid.**

That last one becomes especially important to Joel later.

Clyde and Minnow also teach Joel how to use his crossbow.

And suddenly Joel is at least slightly less helpless.

Clyde even gives him a grenade before they separate.

Which turns out to be extremely useful later.

## Joel Finally Saves Boy

One of my favourite moments is when Boy gets trapped by a giant centipede-like monster.

Normally, this is exactly where Joel would freeze.

And he does freeze for a moment.

But this time he pushes through it.

He uses his crossbow and kills the monster with a shot to the head, saving Boy.

That scene is important because it isn't just Joel killing a monster.

It shows that he has actually changed.

Earlier, Joel was the guy who couldn't move when a monster appeared.

Now he is putting himself in danger to save someone else.

And yes, that someone else happens to be a dog.

But still.

**Character development is character development.**

## Then We Meet Mav1s

Later, Joel and Boy find an abandoned motel and discover **Mav1s**, a damaged service robot.

This part was surprisingly interesting to me.

Mav1s is basically an AI companion in a post-apocalyptic world.

And watching Joel interact with a robot that is trying to help him survive feels strangely relatable in our current AI era.

The robot isn't some evil machine trying to destroy humanity.

It is basically:

**"Hi, I am here to help you."**

And Joel really needs the help.

Mav1s manages to power Joel's radio long enough for him to contact Aimee again.

That moment is important because Joel finally gets confirmation that he is getting closer to her.

Unfortunately, Mav1s' battery is dying.

So even the robot companion has a limited amount of time.

I liked this little section because it gives Joel another type of relationship besides the romance and the dog.

## The Sand Gobbler Queen

Then we get another reminder that the Sand Gobblers aren't finished with Joel.

Joel and Boy encounter the **Queen Sand Gobbler**.

This thing is massive.

Boy accidentally gives away their position, and Joel has to use the grenade Clyde gave him.

He manages to kill the Queen.

But he is furious with Boy afterward.

Boy runs away.

And Joel then has another problem.

He has to cross the water.

Unfortunately, the water is full of poisonous leeches.

And these things attach themselves all over him.

At this point Joel starts hallucinating and is basically on the verge of collapsing.

Luckily, he remembers the survival knowledge he was taught and uses a plant with natural antivenom properties.

I initially remembered this as him eating a flower, but it is actually **fern leaves** that help counter the leech venom.

So once again, the lesson is:

**Clyde wasn't just talking nonsense.**

The survival lessons are actually keeping Joel alive.

## Finally Meeting Aimee

After all that, Joel finally reaches Aimee's colony.

And this is where the movie does something I actually appreciated.

Aimee isn't simply waiting around for Joel.

She has changed.

She has built a life without him.

She is happy to see him, but she doesn't suddenly fall back in love with him just because he walked 80 miles through a monster apocalypse.

I think this was the right choice for the story.

Joel spent years imagining Aimee as the person waiting for him.

But people change.

Life moves on.

And the journey has changed Joel too.

So even though Joel finally reaches the girl he crossed the entire monster-infested country to find, things aren't exactly how he imagined them.

## And Then the Marines Arrive...

Well, sort of.

A group led by **Cap** arrives and claims they are there to take the colony somewhere safer.

At first, this sounds great.

Joel has finally made it.

Aimee has people who can help them.

Everyone might actually get out safely.

Except Joel starts noticing things that don't add up.

Cap isn't exactly what he claims to be.

Joel even recognises that the berries Cap gives him are poisonous because of everything Boy and Clyde taught him.

That is another nice payoff.

The guy who couldn't survive outside the bunker is now noticing things that experienced people are missing.

Eventually the truth comes out.

Cap and his crew are basically pirates, and they are planning to raid the colony.

Worse, their boat is being pulled by a gigantic mutated crab that they control using an electrified chain.

And their plan is to feed the colony to it.

Because apparently being a post-apocalyptic pirate wasn't evil enough already.

## The Giant Crab

The final monster is probably one of the weirdest.

A giant crab.

And I have to admit, it looks pretty impressive.

Joel gets the chance to shoot it.

But then he remembers what Clyde taught him.

Not every monster is hostile.

Look at its eyes.

And Joel realises the crab isn't actually attacking them because it wants to.

It is being controlled.

So instead of killing the crab, Joel cuts the electrified chain.

The crab is freed.

And once it is free, it turns on Cap and his crew, sinks their boat and kills them.

I liked this ending because Joel's biggest improvement isn't that he has become better at killing monsters.

It is that he has become better at **understanding them**.

That's a much more interesting change.

## Joel Doesn't Get the Romance Ending He Expected

After everything is over, Joel and Aimee share a goodbye kiss.

But this isn't really a traditional:

**"We finally found each other and now we're together forever."**

Joel accepts that Aimee has changed.

He also realises that his bunker family matters to him.

So he heads home.

And when he gets back, he tells everyone about Clyde, Minnow, Boy and everything he learned on the surface.

He even starts sharing his survival knowledge over the radio.

That part is probably my favourite part of Joel's character.

He could have kept everything to himself.

Instead, he shares what he learned.

He tells other survivors that they don't necessarily have to stay underground forever.

And that feels like the real payoff of his journey.

## Joel Started as the Guy Who Froze

At the beginning of the movie, Joel freezes when a monster appears.

By the end, he is:

- Fighting monsters.
- Saving Boy.
- Using a crossbow.
- Recognising poisonous food.
- Understanding monster behaviour.
- Making survival decisions.
- Helping other survivors.
- And encouraging people to leave their bunkers.

He doesn't become some unbeatable action hero.

He becomes **more capable**.

And I liked that.

Even Clyde and Minnow are proud of him by the end.

Joel's biggest achievement isn't killing the most monsters.

It's learning enough to survive and then passing that knowledge to other people.

## What I Liked

- Dylan O'Brien fits Joel really well.
- The movie gives Joel an actual character arc.
- Boy is easily one of the best parts of the movie.
- The monsters are creative and genuinely disgusting in a fun way.
- Clyde and Minnow are great additions to Joel's journey.
- The survival lessons actually pay off later.
- Mav1s is a surprisingly interesting AI companion.
- I liked that not every monster is automatically evil.
- Joel learning to understand the creatures is more interesting than simply killing everything.
- The movie balances comedy, adventure and danger pretty well.
- The ending is more about Joel growing as a person than getting the girl.

## What Didn't Work as Much for Me

The biggest thing for me is that Joel's original reason for leaving is a little ridiculous.

I understand wanting to see the person you love.

But walking 80 miles through a world filled with giant monsters when you have basically zero survival experience is... not exactly a great plan.

The movie knows this and makes Joel look hilariously unprepared at first, so it works as comedy.

Still, I couldn't help thinking:

**Mate, maybe bring someone with you?**

I also felt that the Cap storyline was less interesting than Joel's actual journey.

I was much more interested in Joel learning about the monsters, Boy and Clyde and Minnow than I was in the human pirates.

The giant crab is fun, but I think the movie could have worked without quite as much of the Cap subplot.

## Final Thoughts

I actually enjoyed *Love and Monsters* more than I expected.

The movie starts with a guy who is terrified of monsters and has basically no idea how to survive outside his bunker.

Then it sends him on a ridiculous 80-mile journey because he wants to see his old girlfriend.

Along the way he gets attacked by:

- Giant frogs.
- Giant insects.
- Sand Gobblers.
- Leeches.
- A giant crab.
- And basically anything else that looks like it crawled out of the world's worst nature documentary.

But the funny thing is that Joel doesn't really become a hero by becoming stronger.

He becomes a hero by **learning**.

Clyde teaches him.

Minnow teaches him.

Boy warns him.

Mav1s helps him.

And Joel actually listens.

That makes the ending work for me.

He returns to his people and shares everything he learned instead of keeping the knowledge to himself.

And I think that is why I ended up liking Joel.

He is scared, sometimes stupid, occasionally completely hopeless...

but he is also determined and kind.

He doesn't just survive for himself.

He wants other people to survive too.

### My Take

**I liked it.**

It is not a perfect movie, and the human pirate storyline didn't interest me as much as the monster journey.

But Joel, Boy, Clyde and Minnow make the adventure enjoyable.

And the idea of a guy learning how to survive one monster at a time is surprisingly wholesome for a movie where people are regularly being eaten by giant creatures.

Also, if you like Dylan O'Brien from *The Maze Runner*, this is a very different kind of role for him.

Thomas had to escape a maze.

Joel has to cross an entire monster-infested world.

Apparently Dylan O'Brien's characters just aren't allowed to have a normal commute.
