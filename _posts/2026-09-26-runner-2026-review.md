## A Slow Start

*Runner* starts fairly slowly. We first see Hank doing his runner job, taking errands and deliveries to make a living.

Alan Ritchson is a good fit for the role. The guy is already known for playing big, physical characters in *Reacher* and *War Machine*, so watching him as a courier who can also beat people up is pretty natural.

Hank gets a call from a client, accepts the job and does what he is paid to do. At first, it is really that simple.

Then he gets offered a job with a **very big payday**.

And that immediately feels like a red flag.

Big money usually means big risk, and in this case the job is definitely operating in a grey area.

The official premise describes Hank as a former soldier and high-end courier who becomes caught up in a time-sensitive medical delivery with Ben Bishop, played by Owen Wilson. The movie was released in September 2026 and runs about 98 minutes. [Official Runner page](https://www.angel.com/movies/runner)

## The Package

Hank eventually learns that what he is carrying is connected to a child who desperately needs medical help.

Instead of taking the money and walking away, Hank decides to do the right thing and get the package to the hospital so the child can be saved.

I was actually confused at first.

I kept thinking, **"You could have taken the big money. Why are you doing this?"**

But this decision is what turns the movie from a simple delivery job into the main action story.

Unfortunately, doing the right thing comes with a price.

The villain is willing to kill anyone who gets in the way because he wants the package for his own reason: saving his mother.

Once Hank chooses to help the child, the whole situation turns into a running battle.

## And Then Everyone Wants the Package

This is where the movie really gets going.

Hank is chased. People get killed. The package keeps becoming the centre of every conflict, and almost everyone seems to want to get their hands on it.

Owen Wilson's character provides a different energy from Hank, which I liked. Hank is the big physical guy who can fight his way through problems, while Owen Wilson brings more humour and a lighter personality to the partnership.

The combination works pretty well for an action movie like this.

## The Action Is Good, But the Music...

The fight choreography was generally pretty good.

I liked how physical the fights felt, and Alan Ritchson's size makes the action easy to buy. The car chase was also one of the better parts for me.

But there was one thing that really bothered me:

**the music.**

During some of the fight scenes, the music felt noticeably bad and didn't really match what was happening on screen.

Instead of making the action more exciting, it sometimes felt like the soundtrack belonged to a different movie.

That was disappointing because the choreography itself was mostly enjoyable.

## Hank's Real Reason

Later, we learn why Hank is so determined to do the right thing.

He has been dealing with the disappointment and regret surrounding his daughter, who has cancer. He doesn't want to reach the end of his life knowing that he could have helped someone and chose not to.

That made his earlier decision make more sense to me.

At first I was confused about why he would throw away a big payday and put himself in danger.

Later, I understood that he isn't simply trying to be a hero. He is trying to make sure he doesn't have another regret.

## Owen Wilson's Death

One of the saddest parts for me was when Owen Wilson's character gets shot while they are running from the villain.

Even then, he still manages to joke with Hank and tells him to try the right food in Paris.

Then he dies before they reach the hospital.

I honestly felt quite sad about this, and also felt that it was a little unnecessary.

Hank survives so many dangerous situations, while Owen Wilson gets shot and dies before reaching the hospital.

It made me think about plot armour.

Maybe Hank survives because he is the main character.

Or maybe it is because the guy is built like a tank.

Either way, I was more affected by Owen Wilson's death than I expected.

## The Final Hospital Fight

Eventually everything comes down to the hospital.

Hank has to get the package where it needs to go while the villain is still trying to stop him.

Against all odds, Hank defeats the villain and the child is saved.

It is a fairly straightforward ending, but by this point I was already invested in Hank's journey, so I was happy to see him succeed.

The important thing is that Hank followed through on his decision to do the right thing.

## Final Thoughts

*Runner* took a while to get going for me.

The beginning is slow because we spend time seeing Hank doing ordinary runner jobs before the much bigger problem appears. Once he decides to help the child, though, the movie becomes much more entertaining.

I liked the basic idea of a normal delivery turning into a massive chase. Alan Ritchson works well as the physical lead, and Owen Wilson gives the movie some humour and a different personality to bounce off him.

The fight choreography was mostly good, and the car chase was enjoyable.

My biggest complaint was the music during the action scenes. It just didn't always fit what I was watching.

And Owen Wilson's death genuinely made me sad. It felt especially harsh because Hank somehow manages to survive almost everything thrown at him.

Still, *Runner* is a pretty straightforward action movie.

The basic mission is simple:

**deliver the package, save the child, and try not to get killed along the way.**

And sometimes that is enough.

### My Take

I liked it, although it definitely has some problems.

The movie starts slow, but once the chase begins it becomes a decent action ride. The Alan Ritchson and Owen Wilson pairing works, the physical action is mostly solid, and there are some surprisingly emotional moments underneath all the fighting and car chases.

The soundtrack during the action scenes was a miss for me, and Hank's ability to survive everything requires a little suspension of disbelief.

But if you're going into *Runner* expecting Alan Ritchson punching people, Owen Wilson making jokes, cars crashing and one very important package being chased around town, that's basically what you get.
