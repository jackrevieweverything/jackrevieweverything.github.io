## I wasn't even supposed to watch this movie

I watched *Resident Evil* on **17 September 2026 at around 7:45 PM**.

The funny thing is, I wasn't supposed to be watching it that night. My sister was sick, and we had originally planned to watch the Korean movie *Hope* together. Since that wasn't happening, I ended up sneaking out to watch *Resident Evil* instead.

Honestly, after watching it, I'm quite glad I did.

The 2026 film is directed by **Zach Cregger** and stars **Austin Abrams as Bryan**, a medical courier caught in the middle of a nightmare in Raccoon City. Sony describes it as an all-new story within the *Resident Evil* franchise rather than another retelling of one of the games.

And that is probably the thing I liked most about it: it feels connected to *Resident Evil*, but it isn't simply trying to copy a game story.

## The movie gets straight to business

The opening immediately caught my attention.

There was no long explanation or huge setup. We just see Bryan walking into the hospital to deliver the organ. While he's moving through the hospital looking for the doctor, I noticed the camera deliberately showing other people in the background — patients and other ordinary hospital activity.

I liked that.

It made the hospital feel like an actual place rather than just a movie set waiting for zombies to appear.

Then Bryan gets another delivery job, and that's when everything starts going horribly wrong.

## The camera made me nervous

![Bryan surrounded by the snowy night](https://www.soyuz.ru/public/uploads/files/2/7648923/202510051125174fb12d7d68.jpg)

*Another look at Bryan in the middle of the nightmare.*

One thing I started noticing was how the camera sometimes points **straight ahead without warning me what might be coming**.

Instead of constantly showing the audience, "Hey, something scary is behind him," the movie sometimes lets Bryan walk into the unknown.

That gave me the feeling that the film was deliberately bringing back the kind of tension I associate with horror games: you know something could be there, but you don't know exactly when the game — or in this case, the movie — is going to punish you for moving forward.

This isn't something I can really name as one specific horror technique, but it worked on me.

I was watching the background and the edges of the frame, wondering if something was about to jump out.

## Bryan slowly learns how to survive

What I enjoyed most was watching Bryan gradually adapt.

At the beginning, he isn't some super-trained action hero. He's just trying to do his job and get through an increasingly ridiculous situation.

But every time something goes wrong, he learns from it.

There are several little moments that made me feel like I was watching someone actually learn the rules of a *Resident Evil* game.

One example was when he tried to escape and the **padlock on the gate** stopped him. Later, he gets another obstacle when the door is blocked.

Then, near the hospital, Bryan finally uses his gun to shoot the stick holding the door. The door gives way and the infected creatures come crashing through, with the larger creature getting delayed in the chaos.

I actually felt proud of him at that moment.

It sounds silly, but after watching this guy struggle for so long, seeing him finally understand how to use the environment felt like watching a player figure out a puzzle.

That is one of the movie's strengths. The film has been described by critics as using a game-like structure, with Bryan moving through different danger zones almost like separate missions.

## It feels like Resident Evil without retelling Resident Evil

This was probably my favourite part.

The movie clearly understands the game.

There are infected creatures, environmental puzzles, limited resources, strange monsters, escalating encounters and that feeling of moving from one horrible location to another.

But it doesn't need to simply put Leon, Claire or another famous game character into the story and remake an existing game.

Instead, we follow Bryan.

That makes the story feel new while still giving me the feeling that I'm inside the *Resident Evil* universe.

Sony itself describes the movie as an **all-new story**, and that approach worked for me because I wasn't constantly waiting for the film to recreate a famous game scene.

## The infected girl scene actually got to me

There was a point where Bryan had to kill an infected girl.

I didn't feel excited about it.

I actually felt a bit guilty watching him do it.

And then, of course, Bryan gets infected himself.

That made me feel quite sad for him because by this point I'd already watched him struggle through so much. He wasn't suddenly becoming a badass who could solve everything. He was surviving by adapting, improvising and getting lucky.

Now he had a countdown running inside his own body.

That changed the feeling of the rest of the movie for me.

## The vomit trick was disgusting... but smart

One of my favourite Bryan moments was when he used the **vomit/spit from one of the infected creatures** to temporarily deal with the rolling infected monster.

It was disgusting.

But I also thought:

**Okay, Bryan is actually getting good at this.**

He's no longer just running away.

He's learning how these creatures behave and finding ways to use their own biology against them.

That was another moment where I felt like the movie was showing a game mechanic without literally turning itself into a video game.

## Then the ending just hurts

By the time Bryan reaches the hospital, I really wanted him to make it.

He had been bitten, but he was still moving. He had delivered the important material. The scientists were working on the antidote.

For a moment, I thought:

**Maybe he's actually going to survive this.**

Nope.

Bryan doesn't tell the scientists how badly infected he is, and by the time they are close to completing the cure, he has already lost too much control.

He transforms, kills the people who are trying to save him, and the cure ends up falling away during the chaos. The film leaves its ultimate fate uncertain.

That was quite sad to watch.

Bryan spent the entire movie trying to survive, and in the end, survival itself becomes the thing that destroys him.

He technically makes it all the way to the people who can save him.

He just doesn't make it there **in time**.

## What I liked

- The movie gets moving almost immediately.
- Bryan feels like an ordinary person rather than an unstoppable action hero.
- The game-inspired structure actually comes through in the way he moves from one problem to another.
- I liked the environmental obstacles and little survival tricks.
- The camera sometimes made me nervous because it didn't always tell me where the next threat was.
- The movie respects the games without simply retelling one of them.
- Bryan gradually becoming better at surviving was satisfying to watch.
- The ending gave his whole journey a pretty tragic payoff.

## What didn't work as much for me

The movie does become increasingly chaotic as it goes along.

For me, the earlier survival sections were easier to connect with because Bryan was dealing with one problem at a time. By the final stretch, everything is happening at once and the creatures become much more extreme.

I still enjoyed the escalation, but I found the emotional side of Bryan's story more interesting than simply watching the monsters get bigger and crazier.

## Final thoughts

I went into *Resident Evil* expecting a video-game movie, and what I got was something that felt more like **watching somebody play a very bad night of Resident Evil**.

That's probably the best compliment I can give it.

I liked that Bryan wasn't already prepared for this world. Watching him slowly figure things out — what to shoot, when to run, how to use the environment and eventually how to use the infected creatures against each other — made his survival feel earned.

And the ending is probably the part that stayed with me most.

After everything Bryan survives, he finally reaches the cure, only for the infection to take control before he can actually benefit from it. That is a pretty cruel ending for a character I had spent the whole movie rooting for.

**My experience: I liked it.**

Not because it perfectly recreates the games, but because it understood what makes *Resident Evil* fun for me: exploring a dangerous place, never quite knowing what is around the next corner, solving ridiculous problems with whatever is available, and desperately trying to survive long enough to reach the next safe room.

This time, Bryan just never got his safe room.
